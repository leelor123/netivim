## 1. Summary (15 lines)

1. Between 1904 and 1914 the Second Aliyah brought several tens of thousands of Jewish immigrants, mostly from the Russian Empire, who founded Hashomer, Degania and the first labour-Zionist institutions (C1-018 [BK]).
2. In WWI the Ottoman-ruled Palestine became a theatre of war; the Jewish Legion and the NILI network aided Britain (C1-018, C1-019 [BK]).
3. On 2 Nov 1917 Britain issued the Balfour Declaration, favouring a "national home for the Jewish people" in Palestine while safeguarding the rights of existing non-Jewish communities (C1-001 [F]).
4. The League of Nations confirmed the British Mandate in 1922; it incorporated the Balfour text (C1-005 [BK]).
5. The Tel Hai clash of 1 March 1920 became a founding Zionist memory; its details and Trumpeldor's last words are contested (C1-013 [F]).
6. Violence in 1920, 1921 and 1929 drove the Yishuv (Jewish community) to build the Haganah; in 1929 about 133 Jews and at least 116 Arabs were killed (C1-009 [F]).
7. The Palestinian Arab national movement, led largely by notable families and the Mufti Haj Amin al-Husayni, opposed immigration and land transfer (C1-021 [BK]).
8. The Great Arab Revolt of 1936-39 began with a general strike on 19 April 1936 and became a rural insurgency crushed by Britain with heavy force (C1-010 [F], C1-022 [BK]).
9. The Peel Commission (report 7 Jul 1937) first proposed partition; Britain then reversed course in the White Paper of May 1939, capping Jewish immigration at about 75,000 over five years (C1-006 [BK], C1-002 [F]).
10. The Jewish response included "tower and stockade" settlements (57 built Dec 1936-Oct 1939) (C1-017 [F]) and the Special Night Squads (C1-016 [F]).
11. The Holocaust destroyed European Jewry; the Yishuv's WWII contribution included the Jewish Brigade; Aliyah Bet tried to bring survivors past British restrictions (C1-011 [F], C1-035 [BK]).
12. After 1945 three Jewish underground groups (Haganah/Palmach, Irgun, Lehi) fought the Mandate; the Irgun bombed the King David Hotel on 22 Jul 1946, killing 91 (C1-012 [F]).
13. In July 1947 the Exodus, carrying 4,515 people, was seized by the Royal Navy and its passengers sent back to Germany (C1-011 [F]).
14. UNSCOP reported on 3 Sep 1947, recommending partition; the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 181 on 29 Nov 1947 by 33-13 with 10 abstentions (C1-015 [F]).
15. Jewish leaders accepted the plan; the Arab Higher Committee and Arab states rejected it; civil war followed (C1-015 [F]). Chapter 1 ends on the eve of that war.

## 3. Codex candidates (30)

Format: Name (English / Hebrew). Entry. Sources.

**People**

1. **Joseph Trumpeldor (יוסף טרומפלדור).** Russian-born Zionist activist (earlier Zion Mule Corps role [UNVERIFIED]; also described as having worked at Degania, C1-054 [F]) who commanded the defenders at Tel Hai. He was shot on 1 Mar 1920 and died that evening while evacuated. Tradition attributes to him "it is good to die for our country"; this is contested. C1-013 [F], C1-038 [BK].
2. **Sarah Aaronsohn (שרה אהרונסון).** Member of the NILI network, which gathered information for Britain against Ottoman forces. Arrested by Ottoman authorities in Oct 1917 and died days later (death date given as 9 or 10 Oct by different sites; manner of death and the whole entry [UNVERIFIED] at page level). Include only as a cautious persona. Not for quiz.
3. **Haj Amin al-Husayni (חאג' אמין אל-חוסייני / الحاج أمين الحسيني).** Mufti of Jerusalem from 1921 and head of the Supreme Muslim Council; chaired the Arab Higher Committee formed 25 Apr 1936. Zionist narratives emphasize his later wartime ties to Germany; Palestinian narratives emphasize his leadership of national resistance. Both views must be shown. C1-010 [F], C1-021 [BK].
4. **Orde Wingate (אורד וינגייט).** British officer who founded the Special Night Squads in 1938 and worked with Haganah men. Admirers (including many Israelis) saw him as a teacher of mobile warfare; critics cite collective punishment and brutality. C1-016 [F].
5. **David Ben-Gurion (דוד בן-גוריון).** Leader of the Jewish Agency Executive and of Mapai; accepted partition in principle. Called the Irgun "the enemy of the Jewish people" after the King David bombing. C1-012 [F], C1-015 [F].

7. **Chaim Weizmann (חיים ויצמן).** Zionist leader. Role in the Balfour negotiations [UNVERIFIED]; C1-047 [F] reports a claim attributed to him on ending the 1936 strike (Zionist and Palestinian sources differ), so do not state it as fact.

**Units and organizations**

11. **Hashomer (השומר).** Jewish watchmen organization (April 1909 to June 1920, grown from Bar-Giora of 1907) that guarded settlements, aimed to replace Arab guards with Jewish labour, and also carried out retaliatory raids; founders include Israel and Manya Shochat, Yitzhak Ben-Zvi, Rachel Yanait. 12. **The Jewish Legion (הגדודים העבריים).** Jewish battalions of the Royal Fusiliers in WWI, serving in the British campaign in Palestine. C1-019 [BK].

15. **The Irgun (האצ"ל, Etzel).** Revisionist-Zionist underground; carried out the King David Hotel attack, condemned by the Jewish Agency and the 1946 Zionist Congress. C1-012 [F].

18. **Special Night Squads (פלגות הלילה המיוחדות).** Joint British-Jewish patrol units of 1938-39 (about 100 men initially). Praised and criticized. C1-016 [F].
19. **The Jewish Brigade (החטיבה היהודית).** Unit of the British Army formed in 1944 and deployed in Italy. C1-035 [BK].
20. **Arab Higher Committee (הוועד הערבי העליון / اللجنة العربية العليا).** Palestinian national leadership body formed 25 Apr 1936; rejected the 1947 partition. C1-010 [F], C1-015 [F].

## 5. Perspectives

### 5.1 Accepted facts (broad scholarly consensus)
- The Balfour letter of 2 Nov 1917 existed as quoted and promised British support for a "national home" for Jews in Palestine, with a safeguard for civil and religious rights of non-Jewish communities (C1-001 [F]).
- Britain governed Palestine under a League of Nations Mandate from 1922/23 (C1-005 [BK]).
- Jewish immigration, land purchase, and political organization grew sharply under the Mandate, and so did Arab opposition, producing violence in 1920, 1921, 1929 and 1936-39 (C1-009, C1-010 [F]).
- The 1929 violence killed civilians on both sides, including the Hebron and Safed attacks on Jews (C1-009 [F]).
- The revolt of 1936-39 was suppressed by Britain at great cost to the Arab population and its leadership (C1-010 [F], C1-022 [BK]).
- The 1939 White Paper limited Jewish immigration and land transfers (C1-002 [F]).
- Six million Jews were murdered in the Holocaust (C1-035 [BK]); many survivors sought entry to Palestine.
- The Irgun bombed the King David Hotel on 22 Jul 1946, killing 91 (C1-012 [F]).
- UN Resolution 181 passed 33-13-10 (C1-015 [F]).

### 5.2 Zionist / Israeli narrative (in its own terms)
- Jews returning to their ancestral homeland, fleeing persecution and building a society through labour, farming and defence (C1-018 [BK]).
- The Balfour Declaration and the Mandate gave international recognition to a long-standing right (C1-001, C1-005).
- Violence in 1920, 1921, 1929 was incited and aimed at civilians; Hebron 1929 symbolizes this; the Yishuv was forced to organize self-defence (C1-009 [F], C1-018 [BK]).
- Tel Hai symbolizes readiness to defend a position at any cost (C1-013 [F]).
- Tower-and-stockade settlements established facts on the ground and expanded Jewish presence during the revolt (C1-017 [F]).
- The 1939 White Paper betrayed Jews as the Holocaust approached; illegal immigration was a moral necessity (C1-002 [F], C1-024 [BK]).
- Acceptance of partition in 1947 is presented as a painful compromise; Ben-Gurion reportedly called the borders "bad from a military and political point of view" (C1-015 [F]; wording as reported, verify before quoting).
- Within this narrative there is an internal debate on the underground attacks: the Irgun maintained it targeted British administration and gave warnings; Jewish Agency leaders condemned the bombing (C1-012 [F]).
- Israeli scholars (Shapira, Morris, Segev) add self-critical views: e.g. on the Yishuv's use of force and on mythologizing (C1-018, C1-019, C1-020 [BK]).

### 5.3 Palestinian Arab narrative (in its own terms)
- Arabs were roughly 88% of the population at the 1922 census (78% Muslim, 10% Christian; Jews 11%) (C1-056 [F], C1-053 [F]). Rashid Khalidi argues the Balfour Declaration never mentioned Palestinians, and that the Mandate treated them as a "non-Jewish" population with only "civil and religious rights" (C1-048 [F]); that wording does appear in the Mandate text (C1-005 [F], preamble and Art. 2).
- Khalidi attributes British motives mainly to imperial and strategic interests such as protecting Egypt's eastern frontier (C1-048 [F]). (McMahon-Hussein / Sykes-Picot claims: [UNVERIFIED].)
- Immigration and land purchase displaced farmers and fed fears of a Jewish takeover; the Palestinian-oriented Jerusalem Story says the 1936 revolt was a protest against immigration and land sales that hurt the fellahin (C1-045 [F]; land data C1-014 [F]).
- On 1929 ("al-Buraq Uprising"): the Jerusalem Story account stresses the 15 Aug 1929 Betar-led rally with a Zionist flag as a provocation, fears for the holy sites, and says most clashes were between Palestinians and British police and that Arab deaths were likely under-reported; it gives 113 Jews and 116 Arabs killed and 200+ wounded (C1-046 [F]). That differs from the Shaw-based 133 Jews killed (C1-009 [F]); both ranges are recorded in section 4.
- On 1936-39: a grassroots national uprising, a six-month general strike followed by a farmer-led armed phase; Britain's repression killed 3,832 Palestinians and injured 14,000+ per Jerusalem Story (C1-045 [F]); Moussa gives 5,000+ killed, ~15,000 wounded, ~6,000 imprisoned, 100 executed, 2,000+ homes destroyed and describes British collective punishment, aerial bombing of villages and arming of Jewish units (C1-047 [F]); Khalidi estimates 14-17% of adult men killed, wounded, jailed or exiled and says Peel's partition was rejected as "an insult" (C1-048 [F]). Moussa also notes Husseini-Nashashibi rivalry weakened unity (C1-047 [F]); intra-Arab deaths (about 1,200) in C1-010 [F].
- On 1947: Jews were about a third of the population and owned about 6-7% of the land but were allotted about 56% of the territory (C1-015 [F], C1-048 [F]); Arab leaders rejected the plan as violating self-determination (C1-015 [F]).
- Caution: C1-045 to C1-048 are an unsigned lexicon page, an opinion piece, and an interview, not archival evidence. Their figures should be shown as "reported by".
- Arab-Jewish contact and Arab neighbours sheltering Jews in 1929 (Cohen): [UNVERIFIED], removed from usable claims.

### 5.4 British perspective
- Britain saw itself as a mandatory trustee balancing two commitments (Jewish national home; rights of existing communities) under strategic and financial pressure (C1-005, C1-006 [BK]).
- Peel (1937) concluded the Mandate was unworkable and recommended partition (C1-006 [BK]); in 1939 London turned to an Arab-majority independent Palestine within 10 years (C1-002 [F]).
- British police and soldiers were targets in 1944-47 (Lehi, Irgun) and the government called the attacks terrorism; the King David inquest said no one in authority received a warning (C1-012 [F]).
- British repression in 1936-39 is the subject of criticism by British historians (C1-022 [BK], C1-016 [F]).
- Britain referred the problem to the UN in Feb 1947 and abstained on Res 181: [UNVERIFIED].
- The Permanent Mandates Commission unanimously found the 1939 White Paper inconsistent with the Mandate's past interpretation (four members: not in harmony; three: justifiable in circumstances), and the Commons rejected a motion against it 268-179 on 22 May 1939 (C1-049 [F]).
- Peel (1937) judged the Mandate unworkable and proposed partition with transfer of up to about 225,000 Arabs and 1,250 Jews; Britain dropped partition in Nov 1938 after the Woodhead Commission (C1-050 [F]).

## 7. Mission / gameplay hooks (5-7)

No shooting or harming of people. Label meanings: DOCUMENTED basis = the event happened as described; RECONSTRUCTED = fictionalized details around a real event; FICTIONAL COMPOSITE = invented characters representing typical people.

1. **"One Dunam, One Day" (tower and stockade logistics puzzle).** Player manages a prefab-delivery convoy, wooden moulds, gravel, wire and a limited crew to complete a 35x35 m compound with a tower and four sheds before dusk and before a landlord/authority deadline. Basis: DOCUMENTED (57 settlements, prefabrication by Solel Boneh, Ottoman rule on one-night building: C1-017 [F]). RECONSTRUCTED: the timer and the crew. Note the source says they were generally built by day; design as "finish within one day under threat", not "one night" (C1-017 [F]). Do not depict attackers; threat shown as a growing "lookout alert" meter.
2. **"Landing at Night" (Aliyah Bet stealth/avoidance + civilian assistance).** Player is a shore-party volunteer guiding a boat's passengers up a beach while avoiding patrol searchlights and checkpoints; helps children and the elderly. Basis: DOCUMENTED context (clandestine landings, British interception, Atlit, Cyprus: C1-011 [F]); events and people FICTIONAL COMPOSITE. No violence; capture means being sent to a camp, shown as a plain consequence.
3. **"The Last Hour at Tel Hai" (holding a position, non-graphic).** Player coordinates supplies, messengers and the safe evacuation of wounded from Tel Hai to Kfar Giladi; no combat shown. Basis: DOCUMENTED (1 Mar 1920, Trumpeldor evacuated and died: C1-013 [F]). Include the memory-controversy panel showing differing accounts of his last words (C1-013 [F]).
4. **"The Vote" (partition decision/map puzzle).** Player assembles the UNSCOP/Ad Hoc map: balance population, land, access routes and the Jerusalem corpus separatum, and then simulates how delegations vote. Show both Zionist acceptance and Arab rejection with their stated reasons. Basis: DOCUMENTED (33-13-10; ~43%/56%/0.65%: C1-015 [F]); RECONSTRUCTED for the negotiation minigame.
5. **"Quota" (White Paper bureaucracy puzzle).** Allocate 75,000 certificates over five years plus 25,000 refugee places, balancing family, youth and skills categories, and see the consequences for those left out. Basis: DOCUMENTED rules (C1-002 [F]); the individual applicants are FICTIONAL COMPOSITE. Must be handled with sensitivity (see 10).
6. **"Guarding the Wadi" (Hashomer-era night watch, avoidance/observation).** Player on a watch rota notices signs (fire, tracks, a stray animal) and decides whom to alert, with an emphasis on early-warning and de-escalation. Basis: RECONSTRUCTED from C1-018 [BK] description of Hashomer; no real incident.
7. **"Records Room" (Operation Agatha / documents).** A detective-style puzzle in the Jewish Agency or Secretariat offices, sorting archives under the 1946 raid. Present both the raid and the later bombing as historical facts through documents only; no attack playable. Basis: DOCUMENTED context (C1-012 [F]); gameplay RECONSTRUCTED.

## 9. Did You Know? (10)

1. The "overnight" tower-and-stockade settlement is partly myth: the source states they were generally built by day; the one-night practice is recorded later, at Bror Hayil in April 1948. C1-017 [F].
2. An Ottoman-era law still in force under the Mandate held that a building erected in a single night could not be demolished by the authorities. C1-017 [F].
3. Tower-and-stockade buildings were prefabricated by Solel Boneh, the Histadrut's construction arm. C1-017 [F].
4. Traditional accounts of Trumpeldor's last words are disputed; one study claims he in fact uttered a Russian curse. C1-013 [F].
5. The Haganah leadership initially approved the King David operation, then asked for delays, and eventually cancelled it, but the Irgun carried it out. C1-012 [F].
6. Of the 91 people killed at the King David Hotel, 49 were clerks, messengers and hotel or canteen staff, against 21 senior government officials. (Nationality counts in the source do not add up; not used.) C1-012 [F].
7. After the 1929 riots, more Arabs than Jews were tried and convicted of murder: 124 charged and 55 convicted, versus 70 Jews charged and 2 convicted. C1-009 [F].
8. By one analysis of British statistics, about 1,200 of the roughly 5,000 Arab deaths in the 1936-39 revolt were caused by intra-Arab violence. C1-010 [F].
9. Exodus 1947's passengers refused to disembark in France and were taken back to Germany, to DP camps. C1-011 [F].
10. In 1945 Jews owned about 5.7% of the land of Palestine by British statistics; 46% was state domain. C1-014 [F].

## 10. Sensitivity notes

**General rule:** Players never harm anyone. Violence is conveyed through consequences (documents, testimonies, maps, silence, sound design), not through depictions. Distinguish organizations (Haganah, Irgun, Lehi, Arab Higher Committee, British administration) from populations (Jews, Arabs, British civilians). Never use dehumanizing terms. Always name victims' groups.

**Holocaust.** Treat as background: show letters, certificates, a lost-names wall, suitcases, a displaced-persons camp register. No camps, bodies, or perpetrators portrayed. Use facts: six million murdered (C1-035 [BK]). Do not make survivors "mission objects"; give them agency and names (FICTIONAL COMPOSITE, labelled). Avoid framing Aliyah Bet as a game win condition tied to numbers of "rescued"; focus on dignity and choice. Link to Yad Vashem materials by reference only (C1-024).

**1929 massacres (Hebron, Safed).** Present as a historical event with casualty ranges (C1-009 [F]) and with both sides' memory: Jewish loss and community destruction, and Arab grievances about holy places and British force. Show it through testimony cards, a map of evacuation, and, only if later verified, documented cases of Arab neighbours sheltering Jews ([UNVERIFIED] now). Present the Palestinian "al-Buraq Uprising" framing and the Jewish "massacre" memory side by side (C1-046 [F], C1-009 [F]). No scenes of killing; no player role as attacker or as defender in combat. Avoid collective guilt: perpetrators were individuals and mobs, not "the Arabs".

**1936-39 revolt.** Show civilians on both sides, strikes, curfews, collective punishment (house demolitions) and the unit controversies like Special Night Squads (C1-016 [F]). Avoid portraying fighters as heroes or villains; show British repression's costs and intra-Arab violence (C1-010 [F]).

**Underground attacks on civilians and British.** Present King David factually: 91 killed, who they were (Arab, British, Jewish staff), unresolved warning dispute with each side's claims (C1-012 [F]). Show Ben-Gurion's and the Jewish Agency's condemnation next to Begin's justification. Do not make an attack playable; use a document/investigation format instead (hook 7). Treat Lehi and Irgun as organizations that parts of the Yishuv opposed. Include British attacks and detentions in the same frame as the underground's, without equating them.

**Tel Hai and national myths.** Show both the commemorative tradition and the critical scholarship (C1-013 [F], C1-038 [BK]). Do not present Arab or Lebanese villagers solely as attackers; sources describe suspicion over French soldiers and mutual misunderstanding (C1-013 [F]).

**Language.** Use "Mandatory Palestine/Land of Israel" appropriately in context; present Palestinian Arab names in Arabic and Hebrew (e.g., al-Husayni). Avoid "terrorist" or "freedom fighter" as game-voice labels; attribute them to the speakers (British government, Irgun, Jewish Agency) as the sources do (C1-012 [F]).
