## 1. Summary (15 lines)

1. On 29 Nov 1947 the UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 181 (partition into a Jewish state, an Arab state and an international Jerusalem); vote 33-13-10 (C2-050 K; date in C2-001).
2. The Yishuv accepted the plan; the Palestinian Arab leadership and the Arab states rejected it (C2-001, C2-028 K, C2-027 K).
3. A civil war began almost at once (dated 30 Nov 1947 in C2-001): attacks on traffic, bombings, and reprisals by both sides, with an estimated 1,000 killed in the first two months and about 2,000 by end of March (C2-001, citing Gelber).
4. Jerusalem and the road to it became the central problem: convoys were ambushed, Jerusalem's ~100,000 Jewish residents were besieged, and the Yishuv improvised armoured trucks (K - verify, C2-024, C2-033).
5. In April 1948 the Haganah launched Operation Nachshon (5-16 Apr) to open the road (C2-056). In the same weeks came the Deir Yassin killings (9 Apr) and the Hadassah convoy attack (13 Apr) (C2-002, C2-005).
6. By 14 May around 250,000-300,000 Palestinians had fled or been expelled (C2-003). Britain's Mandate ended at midnight 14/15 May (C2-001).
7. The Declaration of Independence was read at the Tel Aviv Museum on 14 May at 16:00, kept secret until hours before (C2-004). Kfar Etzion fell the day before (C2-006).
8. On 15 May Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Iraq and Lebanon entered Palestine (C2-001 for the date; units K - verify, C2-026).
9. Key early battles: Negba, Latrun, the Jewish Quarter's fall on 28 May (C2-016, C2-017); the Burma Road opened Jerusalem's supply line in June (C2-007).
10. Truces: 11 Jun - 8 Jul and from 18 Jul (C2-001); the Altalena affair on 20-22 Jun showed the new state's insistence on a single army (C2-009).
11. Operation Dani (July) included the expulsion of ~60,000 (Morris: ~100,000 refugees) from Lydda and Ramle (C2-001; C2-012 S).
12. Bernadotte, UN mediator, was killed by Lehi on 17 Sep 1948 (C2-010).
13. Operations Yoav, Hiram (Oct), Horev (Dec-Jan) and Uvda (Mar 1949) extended Israeli control to the Negev and Galilee; Uvda reached Eilat on 10 Mar 1949 (C2-058, C2-059, C2-060, C2-061).
14. About 700,000+ Palestinians became refugees (UNCCP: 711,000, Sept 1949) (C2-003); UNGA Res 194 (11 Dec 1948, 35-15-8) addressed return and compensation (C2-011).
15. Armistice agreements (Egypt 24 Feb, Lebanon 23 Mar, Jordan 3 Apr, Syria 20 Jul 1949) drew the Green Line, explicitly not a political border (C2-008).

## 3. Codex candidates (name English / Hebrew)

Each entry is 2-4 factual sentences. "(K - verify)" = from scholarship not re-opened this session.

**People**
1. David Ben-Gurion (דוד בן-גוריון): Chairman of the Jewish Agency Executive and head of the provisional government. He read the Declaration aloud (about 16 minutes) and signed first (C2-004). He ordered the shelling of the Altalena (C2-009). Documented: at a cabinet meeting on 16 June 1948 a consensus formed against the refugees' return while fighting lasted, formalized in a cabinet decision of 28 July (Morris chapter summary and a study of the cabinet protocols, C2-074 S). The widely repeated verbatim quote could not be traced to an original and is DROPPED (see section 4.3).
2. Golda Meir (גולדה מאיר): One of two women who signed the Declaration (with Rachel Cohen-Kagan) (C2-004). Earlier in 1948 she met King Abdullah in secret (K - verify, C2-025).
3. Yigael Yadin (יגאל ידין): Acting IDF chief of staff during the Altalena affair (C2-009). He served as chief of operations in the Haganah (K - verify, C2-021).
4. Yigal Allon (יגאל אלון): Palmach commander; appointed to lead Operation Dani on 7 July (C2-012 S). He later described the Altalena shells as warning shots that struck the ship by accident (C2-009).
5. Yitzhak Rabin (יצחק רבין): Operations officer in Dani; signed the Lydda departure order according to C2-013 (S); tells of Ben-Gurion's order in a 1977 interview (C2-012 S). Morris argues there was no direct order to evacuate Lydda as a city (C2-012 S) - disputed.
6. Mickey Marcus (מיקי מרקוס): American army officer, first general of the IDF in the new era; supervised the Burma Road (C2-007). He was accidentally killed by an Israeli sentry on the night of 10-11 June 1948 (C2-062).
7. Menachem Begin (מנחם בגין): Irgun leader. Disputed the Altalena cargo split (20% for Jerusalem unit) (C2-009). Irgun (with Lehi) carried out the Deir Yassin attack (C2-002).
8. Abd al-Qadir al-Husayni (עבד אל-קאדר אל-חוסייני): Palestinian commander of the Holy War Army around Jerusalem; killed days before the Hadassah convoy attack (C2-005 for timing; details K - verify, C2-024).
9. Hajj Amin al-Husayni (חאג' אמין אל-חוסייני): Mufti of Jerusalem, head of the Arab Higher Committee (K - verify, C2-027). His Nazi-era collaboration and his leadership are debated; the Game should state only the documented role (K).
10. King Abdullah I of Transjordan (המלך עבדאללה הראשון): Sought to annex the Arab part of Palestine; the British supported this (7 Feb 1948 in C2-001). Shlaim argues for an Abdullah-Zionist understanding that stopped short of partition-by-force (C2-025 K) - contested by other historians.
11. John Bagot Glubb "Glubb Pasha" (ג'ון באגוט גלאב): British commander of the Arab Legion; wrote that the full number of dead on the Lydda trek would never be known (C2-015 S; C2-034 K).
12. Folke Bernadotte (פולקה ברנדוט): Swedish UN Mediator. His truce ran 11 Jun - 8 Jul; his September proposals (Negev to Arab side, western Galilee to Jewish state) were rejected; Lehi killed him on 17 Sep 1948 (C2-010). Earlier led the White Buses rescue (~21,000 people) (C2-010).
13. Ralph Bunche (ראלף באנץ'): UN mediator after Bernadotte; brokered the armistice agreements (C2-008).
14. Jacques de Reynier (ז'אק דה ריינייה): International Red Cross delegate at Deir Yassin and in the Hadassah convoy aftermath; his unarmed test convoy led to separation of medical and military convoys (C2-002, C2-005).
15. Chaim Yassky (חיים ירושלמי-יסקי): Director of Hadassah Hospital; died in the convoy attack (C2-005).
16. Fawzi al-Qawuqji (פאוזי אל-קאוקג'י): Commander of the Arab Liberation Army (volunteer force); defeated at Mishmar HaEmek (C2-001 for the battle; command K - verify).

**Units and organizations**
17. Haganah (ההגנה): The Yishuv's main underground defence force, the basis of the IDF (K - verify, C2-028). Approved the Deir Yassin operation (C2-002).
18. Palmach (הפלמ"ח): Haganah's elite strike companies; took part at Deir Yassin and in the Harel Brigade at Latrun (C2-002, C2-007, C2-041).
19. Irgun (אצ"ל): Revisionist underground under Begin; merged into the IDF; clash at the Altalena (C2-009).
20. Lehi (לח"י): Smaller radical underground; killed Bernadotte (C2-010); participated at Deir Yassin (C2-002).
21. Israel Defense Forces (צה"ל): established by ordinance on 26 May 1948 as a single army, absorbing Haganah/Palmach and, after the Altalena, the Irgun (C2-055 K, C2-009).
22. Arab Legion (הלגיון הערבי): Transjordan's British-led army; held Latrun and the Old City; took the Jewish Quarter's surrender (C2-007, C2-016).
23. Arab Liberation Army (צבא ההצלה): Volunteers organized by the Arab League (C2-001, C2-024 K).
24. Givati Brigade (חטיבת גבעתי): Defended the south; ~70 Givati soldiers were at Negba in June (C2-017 S).

**Places**
25. Sha'ar HaGai / Bab al-Wad (שער הגיא / באב אל-ואד): Narrow pass on the Jerusalem road, site of ambushes; today burned-out armoured vehicles line the road as memorials (K - verify, C2-033).
26. Latrun (לטרון): British police fort on the Jerusalem road, occupied by the Arab Legion from 18 May; five Israeli assaults (24 May - 18 Jul) failed; many attackers were newly arrived immigrants, some Holocaust survivors; hence the Burma Road. A 140-truck convoy reached Jerusalem on 19 June (C2-007, C2-062).
27. Burma Road (דרך בורמה): Bypass track built May-June 1948; first attempt overturned a jeep on 30-31 May; completed 14 June; carried nightly supplies to Jerusalem (C2-007).
28. Deir Yassin (דיר יאסין): Village west of Jerusalem; attacked 9 Apr 1948. Death toll estimates 93-254 (see section 4) (C2-002).
29. Kfar Etzion (כפר עציון): Kibbutz in Gush Etzion; fell 13 May 1948; 127 defenders killed per C2-006; re-established in Sept 1967 (C2-006).
30. Negba (נגבה): Kibbutz on the Majdal-Bayt Jibrin road. 2 June: ~70 Givati soldiers and ~70 residents; Israel lost 8 killed, 11 wounded. 12 July: Israel lost 5 killed, 16 wounded. Egyptian losses are Israeli estimates only (~100 and 200-300) (C2-063).
31. Lydda and Ramle (לוד ורמלה): Arab towns taken in Operation Dani; ~60,000 residents expelled (C2-001); Morris says ~100,000 refugees (C2-001).
32. Tel Aviv Museum / Independence Hall (מוזיאון תל אביב / בית העצמאות): 16 Rothschild Blvd, site of the Declaration (C2-004).
33. Old City, Jewish Quarter (הרובע היהודי): Fell 28 May 1948; residents evacuated (C2-016); accounts of the evacuation range from "orderly" (UN observer) to "forced" (C2-016, search summary).
34. Rhodes (רודוס): Armistice talks with Egypt, signed 24 Feb 1949 (C2-008).

**Operations and events**
35. Operation Nachshon (מבצע נחשון): 5-16 April 1948 (text says until 20 Apr), ~1,500 Givati and Harel troops under Shimon Avidan, to open the road to Jerusalem; judged a military success but only 1,800 of 3,000 planned tonnes arrived (C2-056).
36. Operation Dani (מבצע דני): 9-19 July 1948; Lydda and Ramle captured, Latrun not; 50,000-70,000 residents expelled according to the article (C2-057).
37. Operation Yoav (מבצע יואב): 15-22 Oct 1948; Beersheba taken 21 Oct; Bayt Jibrin, 'Ajjur, al-Majdal depopulated in following weeks (C2-058).
38. Operation Hiram (מבצע חירם): 28/29-31 Oct 1948, ~60 hours; Upper Galilee taken; >50,000 expelled per the article (C2-059).
39. Operation Horev (מבצע חורב): 22 Dec 1948 - 7 Jan 1949; Israeli units entered Egyptian territory, withdrew under British and UN pressure; al-Auja later demilitarized (C2-060).
40. Operation Uvda (מבצע עובדה): 5-10 March 1949; Umm Rashrash (Eilat) reached 10 Mar (C2-061).
41. Altalena affair (פרשת אלטלנה): 20-23 June 1948; ~940 volunteers aboard; 3 IDF and 16 Irgun killed per C2-009 (other figures exist).
42. Hadassah convoy (שיירת הדסה): 13 Apr 1948; see section 4 and 10 (C2-005).

**Treaties and resolutions**
43. UNGA Resolution 181 (החלטה 181): partition plan, 29 Nov 1947 (C2-050, C2-001).
44. UNGA Resolution 194 (החלטה 194): 11 Dec 1948; 35-15-8; para. 11 on return/compensation (C2-011).
45. 1949 Armistice Agreements and the Green Line (הסכמי שביתת הנשק, הקו הירוק): four agreements; the Egypt agreement says the line is "not to be construed in any sense as a political or territorial boundary" (C2-008).
46. Declaration of Independence (מגילת העצמאות): signed by 25 present on 14 May (12 absent: 11 trapped in Jerusalem, one abroad) (C2-004).

## 4. Disputed points and figures

**4.1 Casualties of the war**
- Overall dead: C2-001 lists 6,373 killed (about 4,000 soldiers and 2,373 civilians) attributed to Stanley Sandler; Morris is cited at 5,700-5,800; broader range 5,000 to 20,000 including civilians and irregulars. C2-001's text ties the 6,373 to Sandler as an overall figure, while Israeli sources commonly use 6,373 for Israeli dead alone (about 1% of the Jewish population) (K - verify; C2-028, C2-021). Do not publish a split by side until checked against Morris 2008 and the Israeli Ministry of Defense. 
- Arab armies' soldiers: roughly 4,000 killed from Egypt, Jordan and Syria combined per C2-001 (one estimate).
- Early civil war: about 1,000 killed and 2,000 injured in first two months; about 2,000 dead and 4,000 wounded by end of March (Gelber via C2-001).
- Recommendation: show these as ranges with attribution, not as a single number.

**4.2 Refugees (Palestinian)**
- UN Conciliation Commission, September 1949: 711,000 refugees outside Israel (C2-003; original report C2-038 K).
- General figure: about 700,000, or ~80% of the Arab inhabitants of what became Israel; infobox 750,000+ (C2-003).
- Before 14 May 1948: ~250,000-300,000 fled or were expelled (C2-003).
- Stages: ~100,000 upper/middle class left early; more than 250,000 left from Tiberias, Haifa, Safed, Beisan, Jaffa, Acre (C2-001).
- Lydda/Ramle: ~60,000 expelled; Morris ~100,000 resulting refugees (C2-001).
- About 160,000 Arabs remained in Israel, a quarter of them "internal refugees" (C2-003).
- UNRWA defines a Palestine refugee as someone whose normal residence was Palestine 1 June 1946 - 15 May 1948 and who lost home and livelihood in 1948 (operational definition, seen via search summary C2-073 S); about 5.9 million are described as eligible for services (same source). UNRWA's own page was blocked (403) and the Wayback Machine is not reachable from this tool; the number must be re-checked.
- Count of depopulated localities differs by compiler: Morris 1987: 369; Khalidi 1992: 418; Abu Sitta's study: 531 (includes Bedouin tribes) (C2-069). Kadman uses Khalidi's 418 and adds ~98,000 Bedouin uprooted per Khalidi (C2-070).
- Jewish refugees from Arab states: over 260,000 settled in Israel (Morris, via C2-001); other sources give higher totals (K - verify). This belongs in Ch2's epilogue only as parallel context; do not use it to offset.

**4.3 Causes of the exodus**
- Palestinian narrative (Nakba): expulsions, massacres, psychological warfare, destruction of villages and the refusal to allow return (C2-023, C2-027, C2-030, C2-014, C2-043).
- Pappe: planned ethnic cleansing under Plan Dalet (C2-022); critiqued by others.
- Morris: refugee problem "born of war, not by design"; but expulsions occurred in many places, and Israel's leaders welcomed and then blocked return (C2-020, K - verify the exact phrasing; do not quote).
- Gelber: emphasizes flight and breakdown of Arab society along with specific expulsions (C2-024).
- Karsh: Arab leaders' calls and chaos; minimizes Israeli intent (C2-031).
- Current scholarship summary in C2-003: "expulsions and violence, and the fear thereof" are the primary causes in most scholarship; some dispute the label "ethnic cleansing". Additional factors: destruction of villages, psychological warfare, Deir Yassin, crop burning, typhoid linked to alleged well poisoning, and collapse of leadership.
- State policy afterwards: the 16 June 1948 cabinet discussion produced a consensus against return during the war; Shertok favoured a conditional post-war return, added to the 1 Aug government reply (C2-074 S). The Ben-Gurion "return must now be prevented" quote from a secondary page could not be matched to an original citation and is [UNVERIFIED] - do not use it in the game.
- Plan Dalet (finalized 10 Mar 1948, implemented April-May 1948): Khalidi calls it a master plan for conquest; Pappe a blueprint for ethnic cleansing; Morris says transfer thinking was "not tantamount to pre-planning" (as quoted in the article; nuanced); Gelber and Tal see it as largely defensive/territorial; scholars remain divided (C2-064).
- Operation Hiram: more than 50,000 Palestinians expelled according to the article; disputed incidents include Jish (IDF report 150-200 killed incl. civilians), Saliha (60-94), Eilabun (13), Tarshiha (bombardment killed 24); Sa'sa files remain closed; Morris says official reaction to atrocity charges was denial (C2-059).

**4.5 Hadassah convoy** (C2-005): 79 dead (the page's lead says 78 Jews and one British soldier; its casualty section says 79 Jews plus one British soldier then totals 79 - internal inconsistency; 1948 diary: 50 killed); about 20 injured; 23 women among dead. Convoy size 105 (or 130 in diary). Disputes: convoy's status (medical vs. military - Arab side said combatants were among them and the firefight lasted hours; Jewish Agency said grave Geneva violation); British non-intervention until about 4 pm; mass grave counts (47 vs 25 recorded).

UN observer said the Legion evacuated efficiently; other accounts call it expulsion. Synagogues were destroyed after the surrender (K - verify, C2-021).

**4.10 Declaration timeline**: Lead's brief said "34 hours". Fetched record: 12 May vote ended after midnight; 13 May date on invitation; 14 May morning invitations asking arrival at 15:30 and secrecy; 13:50-15:00 final approval of text; ceremony 16:00 (C2-004). The "34 hours" figure is NOT confirmed; use the documented timeline (about 30 hours from the vote's end to the ceremony is an inference).

## 5. Perspectives

**5.1 Accepted facts**
- Res 181 adopted 29 Nov 1947; Jewish side accepted, Arab leadership rejected (C2-001, C2-028).
- The Mandate ended 14/15 May 1948; Israel declared statehood; five Arab armies entered 15 May (C2-001).
- Roughly 700,000+ Palestinians became refugees; most were not allowed to return (C2-003, C2-011).
- Roughly 6,000 Israelis died, about 1% of the Jewish population (K - verify, C2-028).
- Armistice agreements of 1949 established lines "without prejudice" to future boundaries (C2-008).

**5.2 "War of Independence" (מלחמת העצמאות) - Israeli narrative, in its own terms**
- A people accepted a UN plan, was attacked by the neighbouring states and local forces, and survived against odds; the declaration of statehood rescued the Holocaust survivors' future (C2-028, C2-004).
- Casualties: ~1% of the population; massacres such as the Hadassah convoy and Kfar Etzion are part of national memory (C2-005, C2-006). Yom HaZikaron is tied to Kfar Etzion's date (4 Iyar) (C2-006).
- Departure of Arabs: leaders' calls to leave, war chaos, and fear; expulsion in some places (Lydda and Ramle) acknowledged by historians, but not a general policy (C2-024, C2-031, C2-020).
- Israeli debates: the Altalena (state authority), Deir Yassin (Irgun/Lehi vs. Haganah), the Lydda order (C2-009, C2-002, C2-012).

**5.3 "Nakba" (הנכבה, النكبة, "the catastrophe") - Palestinian narrative, in its own terms**
- A native society that formed the large majority of the country's population (K - verify; Jews were about one third in 1947) lost its homeland, its cities (Jaffa, Haifa, Lydda, Ramle, Acre), ~400+ villages (number varies by source; Khalidi's All That Remains documents 418; K - verify, C2-023) and the right to return (C2-023, C2-027, C2-042).
- Massacres (Deir Yassin, Lydda, others), expulsions and the destruction of villages are central; Plan Dalet is read as intent (C2-023, C2-022).
- Refugee camps, UNRWA, and the right of return under Res 194 are living political questions (C2-011, C2-043).
- Palestinian leadership was divided and poorly armed; Palestinians also remember their own losses and defeats, e.g., Hadassah convoy attack context is contested (C2-005).

**5.4 Arab states' perspective**
- Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Iraq and Lebanon framed the entry as intervening to protect Palestinian Arabs after the Mandate's end (C2-026 K). Motives were mixed: Abdullah aimed at annexing the West Bank (C2-001 for British support; C2-025), Egypt and others rivalry with Abdullah (C2-026 K).
- Armies were small, poorly coordinated, and not all fully committed (C2-026, C2-024 K - verify numbers).
- Arab states largely did not grant citizenship to refugees; Jordan did (K - verify, C2-037).
- After 1948 several regimes fell (e.g. Egypt 1952, Syria coups 1949) (K - verify).

**5.5 UN / international**
- UN created the partition plan, mediators (Bernadotte, Bunche), truces (SC Res 50, 54), and Res 194 (C2-010, C2-052, C2-053).
- Britain withdrew and was accused by both sides of favouring the other (C2-005 for Hadassah criticism; C2-001 for the Transjordan support).
- US recognized Israel de facto minutes after midnight on 15 May; USSR de jure (K - verify) (C2-004).

## 9. Did You Know? (12 verified facts)

1. Sherf delivering the typed text hitched a ride and arrived at the museum at 15:59, a minute before the ceremony (C2-004).
2. Invitations to the ceremony were delivered by messenger on the morning of 14 May and asked guests to keep it secret (C2-004).
3. Twelve of the 37 council members could not sign: eleven were trapped in besieged Jerusalem and one abroad (C2-004).
4. The US gave de facto recognition eleven minutes after midnight (C2-004).
5. The Burma Road's first night ended with the lead jeep rolling over; the road was completed on 14 June (C2-007).
6. Planners routed the road out of sight of the Latrun fort and laid water and fuel pipelines beside it (C2-007).
7. Mickey Marcus is the subject of the film "Cast a Giant Shadow" (1966), which includes the Burma Road (C2-007).
8. Kfar Etzion's remains lay unburied about 18 months until Rabbi Shlomo Goren buried them on Mount Herzl in Nov 1949 (C2-006).
9. Kfar Etzion was re-established in September 1967 by the children evacuated in 1948 (C2-006).
10. Bernadotte's earlier White Buses rescue saved about 21,000 people from camps in 1945 (C2-010).
11. The Red Cross's unarmed test run to Mount Scopus led both sides to separate medical and military convoys (C2-005).
12. The Altalena carried about 940 volunteers when the government shelled it (C2-009).

## 10. Sensitivity notes

General rules for Ch2: no depiction of killing, bodies, or torture. Use text, sound, objects and aftermath scenes (a shoe, a door left open). Always name perpetrators as organizations ("Irgun and Lehi fighters", "Arab Legion soldiers", "armed local men"), never as a nation. Show the human scale on both sides in equal dignity.

- **Deir Yassin**: Present as an event with a range of death tolls (section 4.4) and note that the first figure of 254 was later considered exaggerated (C2-002). Mention that the Haganah approved the operation but was shocked by the outcome (K - verify). Show the village before (daily life) and the survivors' testimony in text; do not reenact. Include Israeli, Palestinian, and Red Cross sourcing (C2-002). Explain that it was used afterward in propaganda and as a motive for revenge (C2-005).
- **Hadassah convoy**: Show doctors, nurses and the hospital's mission. Do not depict the attack; present the aftermath as a loss to the Yishuv and as a controversy over the convoy's status (C2-005). Point out that it was in part driven by revenge for the death of al-Husayni and Deir Yassin per the page - presented as a claim (C2-005). Acknowledge Red Cross and British actions.
- **Kfar Etzion**: Honor the defenders and the four survivors (C2-006). Present all four versions (Israeli, Arab Legion, Arab, Morris) as contested accounts and say that the fate of those who had laid down arms is disputed. Include the later reconstruction by children in 1967 but mark that settlement in the West Bank after 1967 is a matter of ongoing political dispute (C2-006).
- **Lydda and Ramle**: Show the heat, the walk, the water, the carts - no deaths onscreen. Say outright that residents were forced out by Israeli forces (C2-012, C2-015, C2-001) while noting that scholars debate who gave the order and how many died (section 4.7). Use Israeli (Morris, Rabin) and Palestinian (PalQuest) voices together.
- **Nakba**: Use the term Nakba as the Palestinian name and the "War of Independence" as the Israeli name; give both. Give the number range (section 4.2). Explain causes as debated, with the current majority scholarly view stated and attributed (C2-003). Include the later refugee situation without turning it into modern politics. Do not equate or net out the Jewish refugees from Arab states against the Palestinian refugees. Avoid dehumanizing language for any group.
- **Jewish Quarter**: Treat the evacuation with the same restraint; both "orderly" and "forced expulsion" descriptions exist (C2-016 search summary).
- **Armed forces vs. civilians**: Distinguish the Arab Legion, the Arab Liberation Army and local irregulars from Palestinian civilians; and distinguish Haganah/IDF from Irgun/Lehi and from Jewish civilians.
